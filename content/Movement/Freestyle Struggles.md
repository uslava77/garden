# 1) Vocab
The first hurdle is lack of vocab, or [moves](Dance%20is%20Continuous.md#Move)

Even basic breaking moves can be physically challenging to beginners and take time to learn.
You may have to spend a lot of time learning a sweep, baby freeze, and back-spin.
After all this time, you are left with only three moves.

If there is no vocab to draw from, there is inherently a lack of freedom.

# 2) Connectivity
Vocab is useless if there is no connection points between moves.  
This is the second hurdle.

After learning a top-rock, foot-work, power, freeze: we must learn how to put it all together. 

## Move transitions
We might learn a bruce-lee go down from tops to footwork, or a sweep to windmill, backspin to shoulder freeze, etc.  
Transitions is how we will flow ***smoothly*** from one move to another.

## Combos
We might come across transitions between moves that we enjoy, and decide to call it a [Combo](Dance%20is%20Continuous.md#Combo).  
A distinct unit in our minds rather than separate moves.  
This helps reduce mental load as well.

# Forks in the Road
Each journey begins the same: you are not yet on the dance floor.
After this, the forks in the road emerge.  
At a basic glance folks will typically consider developing many options in:
- Top rock steps
- Go-downs
- Fotwoork steps
- Powermoves
- Freezes

However, because time is continuous there are infinite sub-steps to drill into:
- How you enter the circle before top rock
- How you transition between different top-rock steps
- How you transition between top-rock steps into go-down
- etc.

Imagine taking a picture at random times in your round, from this position do you have options? Or do you always do 'X'?
# Learning challenges
When we first start dancing we often face these challenges:
- Lack of vocab "lack of choice"
- Lack of connectivity "lack of ability to choose"

Vocab is typically learned piece-by-piece (ex. six step, baby freeze). This is often the initial hurdle as even 'basic' movements are physically and technically challenging in breaking.

Connectivity is often not typically taught, or if taught then it is by choreography. It is left as an exercise to the dancer to figure out how to piece together vocab.
