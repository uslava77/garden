# Me vs. World
Our bodies are made of the same stuff as everything else, and bound by physical law which we have no control over. 

In this sense, I have no free will over what the atoms in my body do.
Because we are the sum of our parts, this applies to thoughts, decisions, behavior, etc.
# Predictability
Typically more data about the state gives increasingly accurate predictions.

This points to [Laplace's demon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace%27s_demon).

However, at a small enough scale we see irreducible quantum randomness.
There is no additional data that can be obtained for prediction.
We can only predict via probabilities what the outcome may be.
See [Bell's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell%27s_theorem#:~:text=Bell's%201964%20theorem%20requires%20the,reality%20corresponding%20to%20that%20quantity.%22) and [Heisenberg's uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle).

Thus, even with theoretically sufficient compute, *we* would not be able to deterministically predict the behavior of my being, only probabilistically.

I don't think this disputes determinism, simply that *we* are fundamentally limited.
We'd need a measurement that is not a measurement. Information that is not information. "God"
# Atemporal View
Most physical laws don't indicate a preference for time direction.
The predictions can go forward *or* backward from the initial state.

We are used to it going forward, but this could be an accident.

# Causality
One abstraction we use to understand the world is cause and effect.
We answer the question **why** did an effect occur by attributing a prior cause.

Sometimes we're satisfied with a higher level description: "I fell because I slipped on ice".
Sometimes we drill deeper: "thin liquid layer on solid ice reduces friction"

Cause comes *before* effect, yet if we reverse time *effect* comes before *cause*.
If physical law allows for time-reversal, we can't say one 'caused' the other.
They are simply states consistent with each other as described by physical law.

To me, this aligns with the [delayed choice experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeler%27s_delayed-choice_experiment) and [quantum eraser experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_eraser_experiment).
Our intuition, limited by moving 'forward' in time and the illusion of free will, fails to coincide how a 'decision' to measure one way or another seemingly changes the behavior from particle to wave.

Allowing for an atemporal view, we can simply say that these are states in time consistent with physical law.
